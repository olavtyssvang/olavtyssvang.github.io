Research

Discrete geometry

I enjoy thinking about finite elements from a geometric point of view, as algebraic objects held together by constraints imposed on restriction maps. This perspective was made popular by the theory of finite element systems. Therein they effectively treat finite elements as discrete sheafs, and this has many advantages.

I've been doing some work on this kind of stuff with Boris. It seems potentially lucrative to borrow more ideas from algebraic geometry. Therefore I am currently working my way through Ravi Vakil's book The Rising Sea, which is really great.

Finite elements

At this point our collective understanding for discretisations of vector fields is mostly satisfactory, but there is still much unknown about tensor finite elements. This up-and-coming field, which Kaibo and Ralf has dubbed "Finite Element Tensor Calculus", will no doubt see a lot of activity in the coming years. I inted to join this effort. Specifically, I am interested in the point of view of form-valued forms (see also Finite element spaces of double forms).
